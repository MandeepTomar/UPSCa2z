Why Science & Technology Scores Well

Applied, not theoretical

UPSC tests everyday application of science — how a vaccine works, why a satellite stays in orbit — not exam-style physics or chemistry problems.

Highly current-affairs linked

A large share of questions are pegged to a recent ISRO launch, a new drug, or a tech policy — reading the news carries most of the weight.

Concept once, useful forever

Core scientific principles (immunity, orbits, genetics) don't change year to year, so early preparation keeps paying off.

Scoring section if maintained

Aspirants who read a monthly science current-affairs digest consistently find this one of the highest-accuracy sections in the paper.

How UPSC Actually Asks Science & Technology

Knowing the answer isn't enough — you need to recognize the question format. Here's how UPSC typically frames Science & Technology questions in Prelims.

Statement-based

Two or three statements on a technology, discovery, or organism, asked "which is/are correct" — the most frequent Science format.

Working-principle questions

Tests whether you understand how something works (mRNA vaccine, CRISPR, quantum computing) rather than just its name.

Match the following

Pairs a mission, disease, or material with its correct feature or agency — precision over general familiarity.

Current mission/discovery linked

A recent ISRO mission, Nobel Prize, or health breakthrough is the hook; the answer usually needs a static science fact.

Elimination via extreme claims

One statement often makes an absolute or exaggerated claim about a technology's capability — a common trap to spot.

Application-based scenario

Describes a real-world use case and asks which technology or principle explains it best.

💡 How to think

Treat every ISRO mission, vaccine platform, or new material in the news as a mini static topic — learn its full name, purpose, and one key fact, not just its headline name.

UPSC Favourite Traps

These are the confusions UPSC leans on most often to convert an easy question into a tricky one. Knowing them in advance removes a large chunk of avoidable mistakes.

⚠ Vaccine vs Antibody vs Antigen

A vaccine introduces an antigen to trigger the body's own antibody response — options often swap which one is administered vs which one is produced.

⚠ DNA vs RNA

DNA is double-stranded and stores genetic information; RNA is typically single-stranded and helps express it — mRNA vaccines are a recurring test case.

⚠ Fission vs Fusion

Nuclear fission splits heavy nuclei (used in current reactors); fusion combines light nuclei (used in the Sun, and experimental reactors like ITER).

⚠ ISRO vs NASA vs Private Players

Questions often test which agency or company led a specific mission — a common mix-up between ISRO, NASA, and now private players like SpaceX.

⚠ Renewable vs Non-renewable

Some options mislabel geothermal or tidal energy — always check whether the source naturally replenishes within a human timescale.

⚠ Endemic vs Epidemic vs Pandemic

Endemic is a constantly present disease in a region; epidemic is a sudden regional spike; pandemic is global spread — precise scope matters.

⚠ Stem Cells — Totipotent vs Pluripotent

Totipotent cells can form an entire organism; pluripotent cells can form almost any cell type but not a whole organism — a fine distinction UPSC likes.

⚠ GM Crops vs Hybrid Crops

GM crops involve direct gene transfer (often across species); hybrid crops result from cross-breeding within compatible species — commonly confused.

⚠ Antibiotics vs Antivirals

Antibiotics act on bacteria; antivirals act on viruses — using one term for the other is a frequent factual trap.

⚠ Absolute words & assumptions

Words like "only," "first ever," or "cannot" describing a technology's scope usually hide an exception.

Most Important Facts & Terms

A small set of terms and facts account for a disproportionate share of Science & Technology questions. These are generally worth memorizing precisely, not just recognizing.

ISRO
Indian Space Research Organisation
Headquartered in Bengaluru; missions like Chandrayaan, Gaganyaan, and Aditya-L1 are recurring exam favourites.
CRISPR-Cas9
Gene-editing technology
Allows precise editing of DNA sequences; its discoverers won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
mRNA Vaccine
Messenger RNA vaccine platform
Teaches cells to make a protein that triggers immune response, without using a live virus — used in COVID-19 vaccines.
DRDO
Defence Research and Development Organisation
India's principal defence R&D agency; missile systems (Agni, BrahMos) are frequently tested.
Genome India Project
National genome sequencing initiative
Aims to build a reference genome database of India's diverse population for precision medicine.
Green Hydrogen
Hydrogen produced via electrolysis using renewable power
Central to India's National Green Hydrogen Mission; production method distinguishes it from grey/blue hydrogen.
