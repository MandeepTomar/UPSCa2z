  8. 8 2019

    1. AChalukya

  13. 13 2014

    With reference to the cultural history of India, the term ‘Panchayatan’ refers to

    1. Aan assembly of village elders
    2. Ba religious sect
    3. Ca style of temple construction
    4. Dan administrative functionary
    Reveal answer

    ✓ Correct answer: C. a style of temple construction

    Explanation

    Panchayatan is a style of temple architecture in which the main shrine is surrounded by four subsidiary shrines at the corners of a square/rectangular plinth, a layout popularised in the Gupta period (e.g., Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh).

  16. 16 2013

    With reference to the history of Indian rock-cut architecture, consider the following statements:
    1. The caves at Badami are the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India.
    2. The Barabar rock-cut caves were originally made for Ajivikas by Emperor Chandragupta
    Maurya.
    3. At Ellora, caves were made for different faiths.
    Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. A1 only
    2. B2 and 3 only
    3. C3 only
    4. D1, 2 and 3
    Reveal answer

    ✓ Correct answer: C. 3 only

    Explanation

    The Barabar caves near Gaya, patronised by Ashoka (not Chandragupta Maurya) for the Ajivikas, and Ellora, which has Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain caves side by side, support statement 3 as correct.   17. 17 2013

    Some Buddhist rock-cut caves are called Chaityas, while the others are called Viharas. What is the difference between the two?

    1. AVihara is a place of worship, while Chaitya is the dwelling place of the monks
    2. BChaitya is a place of worship, while Vihara is the dwelling place of the monks
    3. CChaitya is the stupa at the far end of the cave, while Vihara is the hall axial to it
    4. DThere is no material difference between the two
    Reveal answer

    ✓ Correct answer: B. Chaitya is a place of worship, while Vihara is the dwelling place of the monks

    Explanation

    A Chaitya is a Buddhist prayer hall or shrine centred on a stupa, used for worship, while a Vihara is a monastery or residential hall where monks lived and studied.

  19. 19 2012

    Lord Buddha's image is sometimes shown with the hand gesture called 'Bhumisparsha Mudra'. It symbolizes

    1. ABuddha's calling of the Earth to watch over Māra and to prevent Māra from disturbing his meditation
    2. BBuddha's calling of the Earth to witness his purity and chastity despite the temptations of Māra
    3. CBuddha's reminder to his followers that they all arise from the Earth and finally dissolve into the Earth, and, thus this life is transitory

    Reveal answer

    ✓ Correct answer: B. Buddha's calling of the Earth to witness his purity and chastity despite the temptations of Māra

    Explanation

    The Bhumisparsha (earth-touching) Mudra depicts the Buddha calling the earth to bear witness to his enlightenment and his resistance to the temptations of Mara, symbolising steadfastness and purity rather than a request for protection.

    ✓ Correct answer: D. These are parts of architectural structures

    Explanation

    Ashokan pillars were monolithic, highly polished, and had a characteristic tapering shaft, but unlike later pillars they were free-standing, independent monuments rather than parts of larger architectural structures.
